Research Paper Overview: Semantic Self-Consistency

Algoverse AI Research

Paper: "Semantic Self-Consistency: Enhancing Language Model Reasoning via Semantic Weighting"
Authors: Tim Knappe*, Ryan Li, Ayush Chauhan, Kaylee Chhua, Kevin Zhu, Sean O'Brien (Algoverse AI Research)

Standard self-consistency (Wang et al., 2022) generates multiple chain-of-thought rationales and takes a simple majority vote over the final parsed decisions, discarding the rich reasoning steps. Knappe et al. hypothesize that language models often execute valid reasoning steps but fail on final arithmetic/symbolic synthesis. By featurizing entire reasoning paths into dense semantic vector representations, responses can be dynamically weighted and outliers filtered before taking the majority vote.

1. Centroid Proximity Weighting (CPW)

Embeds all $N$ reasoning paths into vector space, determines the geometric centroid, and weights each candidate inversely proportional to its Euclidean distance to the centroid.

centroid = (1 / N) * sum(e_i)
d_i = ||e_i - centroid||_2
norm_d_i = d_i / sum(d_j)
w_i = 1 / norm_d_i
Score(answer_u) = sum(w_i for i in I(u))

Candidates clustered closely in reasoning space receive higher voting weights; reasoning paths far from the center are marginalized.

2. Semantic Consensus Weighting (SCW)

Computes full pairwise cosine similarities across all candidate rationale embeddings, assigning each rationale a consensus score based on its total similarity to all peers.

sim(e_a, e_b) = (e_a . e_b) / (||e_a||_2 * ||e_b||_2)
S(e_i) = sum(sim(e_i, e_j) for j in N)
Consensus_Vote(answer_u) = sum(S(e_i) for i in I(u))

Ranks higher those chains of reasoning that share maximum semantic alignment across the generated cohort.

Detects degenerated reasoning, loops, and hallucinations before voting by pruning anomalous rationale vectors using three algorithms with optimal paper hyperparameters (Appendix I.2):
