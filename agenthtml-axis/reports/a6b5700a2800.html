Algorithm Map

The paper replaces likelihood evaluation with a conditional score model trained on simulator pairs and then samples via reverse diffusion or a probability-flow ODE.

Simulate pairs

Draw parameters from the prior or proposal and run the simulator to form (θ, x) data.

Noising process

Diffuse θ with VE or VP SDE transition densities whose conditional scores are known.

DSM training

Train spsi(θt, x, t) against denoising score matching targets.

Posterior sampling

Use the learned score inside the reverse SDE or probability-flow ODE at x_obs.

Sequential focus

TSNPSE defines truncated prior proposals using high-probability regions of the current posterior.

Preferred Sequential Variant

TSNPSE avoids explicit proposal-posterior corrections when the retained highest-probability region covers the true posterior support. Each round trains on accumulated simulations and updates a truncated proposal.

round r:
  sample theta from truncated prior proposal
  simulate x from p(x | theta)
  train posterior score network by DSM
  sample approximate posterior at x_obs
  estimate HPR_epsilon and update truncation

Experimental Surface

A faithful reproduction would need the eight sbibm benchmarks, several simulation budgets, and the pyloric neuroscience problem with invalid-summary handling.

Benchmarks and Metrics

The paper evaluates eight SBI tasks: Gaussian Linear, Gaussian Mixture, Two Moons, Gaussian Linear Uniform, Bernoulli GLM, SLCP, SIR, and Lotka Volterra.

Budgets are 1,000, 10,000, and 100,000 simulations. Reported quality uses C2ST where 0.5 is best. NPSE is compared with NPE; TSNPSE is compared with SNPE-C and TSNPE.

C2ST VE SDE VP SDE RK45 ODE

Training Recipe

  • Separate 3-layer MLP embeddings for θt and x.
  • 64-dimensional sinusoidal t embedding.
  • 3-layer 256-unit SiLU score MLP.
  • Adam at 1e-4 with 15 percent validation holdout.
  • Early stopping after 1000 non-improving validation steps.

Neuroscience Target

The pyloric network experiment infers 31 conductance and synapse parameters from 18 summary statistics derived from 3 voltage traces. The paper reports that more than 99 percent of prior samples can produce ill-defined summaries, making sequential proposals central to efficiency.

The reported TSNPSE setup uses 9 rounds, with 30,000 initial simulations and 20,000 added simulations per round. The paper states the final round reached 81 percent valid summary statistics.
