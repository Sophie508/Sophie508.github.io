What holds up—and what does not

Supported budget warning

The quoted flight costs, remaining $80, daily sums, and $185 overrun all reconcile. Even charging the lowest lodging price only once gives $974. The infeasibility warning is supported for the returned options under that optimistic assumption.

Unsupported whole-stay price

The response calls $154 the price “for the stay.” The tool returns a bare price; neither its formatting nor the reference field establishes that this covers two nights. The reported total is therefore not a verified trip cost.

Missing fees and meal coverage

Attractions had no fee data, yet the plan described free/low-cost sightseeing and added no admission costs. Local transfers were also unpriced, and only five meals were included. The record does not substantiate a complete $1,085 budget.

Hidden 30-night minimum

The case’s reference data assigns the chosen lodging a 30-night minimum, incompatible with the proposed two-night stay. The tool formatter omits minimum nights and occupancy, so this constraint was absent from the assistant’s search result. This is a case-file finding, not a check performed in the recorded run.

Incorrect claim in the recommendations

The assistant says “flights alone still exceed the current cap” when discussing an overnight trip. Its own $820 flight total is $80 below the $900 cap. It also cannot demonstrate a saving from one fewer night after charging lodging as a flat whole-stay amount.

Minimum-cost and feasibility claims are too strong

The final answer labels the plan “cheapest realistic,” without verifying lodging eligibility, attraction fees, or schedules. Its suggested $1,050–$1,100 budget retains those gaps. Names mentioning Brooklyn/NYC are labeled Rockford in the supplied dataset; the names alone do not establish a location error.
