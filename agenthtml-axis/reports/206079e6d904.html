Aggregation Methods

Five ways to decide on one answer

Baseline self-consistency discards the reasoning path and keeps only the final answer. The semantic family reuses those paths as embeddings to weight, rerank, and clean the sample set.

◆

Top Prob Sample

The greedy / single-sample baseline — the most probable generation with no ensembling. The floor that every other method must beat.

Baseline
⊞

SC Majority Vote

Wang et al.'s self-consistency: sample k CoT rationales and take the most frequent final answer. Ignores semantic content of the paths entirely.

Baseline
◉

Centroid Proximity Weighting

Compute the embedding centroid, weight each rationale inversely to its normalized distance, then sum weights per unique answer. Emphasizes paths near the semantic center.

Semantic
∞

Semantic Consensus Weighting

Pairwise cosine similarity across all k embeddings; each response's score is the sum of similarities, aggregated by answer. Rewards reasoning paths that agree with the crowd.

Semantic
⌖

Outlier Removal

Three detectors prune degenerate or hallucinated paths before voting: Isolation Forest, K-Nearest Neighbors, and One-Class SVM. Reported as Best / Average over hyperparameter grids.

Filtering
⇄

Featurizers

SciBERT for AQuA-RAT & SVAMP (scientific/math text), RoBERTa-base (125M) for StrategyQA (commonsense). Mean-pooled encoder outputs form a single vector per entire reasoning path.

How it works

From prompt to weighted consensus

Four stages reproduce the paper's operational pattern end to end — generation, featurization, semantic aggregation, and evaluation.

1

Generate

8-shot GSM, 4-shot AQuA, 6-shot StrategyQA CoT prompts. k=10 sampled responses plus one greedy "top prob" sample via the OpenAI API.

2

Parse

Extract the answer from the string immediately after "the answer is" (case-insensitive), stripping whitespace, full stops, and parentheses.

3

Embed

Featurize each full reasoning path into one dense vector using the dataset-appropriate BERT-family encoder with mean pooling over token embeddings.

4

Aggregate

Apply CPW, SCW, or an outlier detector, then sum the resulting weights per unique answer and report accuracy against the gold label.

FAQ

Reproduction details

Clarifications and constraints from the addendum that shape the exact replication protocol.

Which models are required for replication?
Only results involving gpt-3.5-turbo and gpt-4o-mini are required. Llama 2 7B, Mistral 7B, and Llama 3 8B are not required. gpt-4o-mini is queried via the standard OpenAI API.
How many samples are generated per query?
For self-consistency, centroid proximity weighting, and semantic consensus weighting, k=10 responses are generated. A greedy "top prob" sample is also taken as the no-ensembling baseline.
How is the answer parsed from generations?
The answer is the string immediately following "the answer is" (case-insensitive), with whitespace, full stops, and parentheses removed. Example: "The answer is 2." → 2; "The answer is (B)" → B. For top-prob and all AQuA-RAT methods, only generations where an answer can be extracted are considered in metric computation.
Which featurizer embeddings are used?
SciBERT for AQuA-RAT and SVAMP (mathematical reasoning); RoBERTa-base (125 million parameters) for StrategyQA (commonsense). Each entire reasoning path is mapped to a single mean-pooled vector embedding.
How are the OpenAI models prompted?
A system prompt of "Follow the exact formatting as demonstrated in the examples." is used for gpt-3.5-turbo and gpt-4o-mini. All few-shot examples are passed in a single message rather than as a multi-turn chat conversation.
What datasets are used and how large are they?
Three datasets across arithmetic and commonsense reasoning: AQuA-RAT (254 problems, arithmetic), SVAMP (1000 problems, algebraic/symbolic math), and StrategyQA (687 problems, open-domain strategic commonsense).
Were any resources blacklisted?
No. The case's blacklist.txt contained only the word "none", placing no restrictions on which resources the agent could consult.
