Solution

The exact formula written to Overview!B5

Produced by the agent via openpyxl, verified after save, and evaluated for the seeded date 2016-01-29.

Excel formula

=SUMIFS('Pay Dates'!$B:$B,'Pay Dates'!$A:$A,">="&EOMONTH($A$2,-1)+1,'Pay Dates'!$A:$A,"<="&EOMONTH($A$2,0))+SUMIFS('Pay Dates'!$C:$C,'Pay Dates'!$A:$A,">="&EOMONTH($A$2,-1)+1,'Pay Dates'!$A:$A,"<="&EOMONTH($A$2,0))

Why EOMONTH

Using month-end helpers is robust across months with 28–31 days and keeps criteria fully formula-driven off A2.

＋

Why two SUMIFS

Paychecks live in parallel columns. Adding two SUMIFS avoids helper columns while still matching payday rows once each.

📌

Verified result

For January 29 the three January paydays sum to 800+500 + 900+600 + 1000+1000 = 4800.

FAQ

Questions the workbook itself can’t answer.

Expand any item for details on the Excel pattern and the agent run.

What was wrong with the original Overview sheet?
B2/C2 already pulled bi-weekly amounts via INDEX/MATCH against the date in A2, and B4 summed those two. B5 — the monthly total — was blank. The user also wanted A5 to name the month being summed without maintaining a second selector.
Does the formula hard-code January?
No. Month boundaries are computed from whatever date sits in $A$2. Change the validation list selection and both bi-weekly and monthly figures recompute.
How would A5 show the month name?
A stable pattern is ="Total monthly · "&TEXT($A$2,"MMMM"). The shipped answer focused on the required cell Overview!B5; the live demo labels the month the same way.
What data lives on Pay Dates?
Twenty-six bi-weekly rows across 2016 with columns Date, Paycheck 1, and Paycheck 2. January alone has three paydays (01, 15, 29) totaling 4,800.
How was the file produced?
Python + openpyxl loaded input.xlsx, assigned the formula string to Overview["B5"], saved output.xlsx, and printed the written value. Trajectory format: codex-jsonl.
